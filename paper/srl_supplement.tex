\noindent
This supplement lists the parameters of the synthetic catalogues used in the
worked example of the main paper (Table~\ref{tab:s_generator}) and collects
additional screen captures of the \emph{Catalogue of Catalogues} (CofC) platform
that support, but are not essential to, the argument of the main paper.  All
captures are from the release described in the main paper, running in a browser
against a local instance.

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{Data.}  Every capture uses a reduced-scale version of the worked
example's synthetic pair, built by the same seeded generator
(\path{paper/figures/synthetic_catalogues.py}, seed~42) with each true
earthquake kept with probability $1/23$: a $4{,}922$-event ``GeoNet-like''
catalogue and a $4{,}411$-event ``Agency~B'' catalogue, each uploaded as a
QuakeML~1.2 file written by
\texttt{python}~\path{paper/figures/generate_figures.py}
\texttt{-{}-screenshot-catalogues}.
The values quoted in the captions are those of this reduced pair, computed by
the platform's own code (\path{paper/figures/screenshot_expectations.ts}, which
parses the files with the upload parser and drives the Analysis page's analysis
worker); the worked example of the main paper uses the full-scale pair.  No real
event data are shown anywhere in this supplement.

  $45.6^{\circ}$S), 110/45~km ($25^{\circ}$), 5~km plus an exponential tail of
  scale 25~km (at most 150~km); distributed offshore 5\%, uniform over the
  domain, 5--30~km. \\
Aftershocks & Sequences on every background event of $M \geq 4.5$, sized so that
  23\% of the true events of $M \geq 2.0$ are aftershocks and shared between
  sequences in proportion to $10^{0.8(M - 4.5)}$ (Utsu, 1970); times from the
  modified Omori law ($p = 1.1$, $c = 0.05$~d, up to 365~d;
  Omori, 1894; Utsu, 1961); epicentres uniform in a disc of radius twice the
  rupture length $\log_{10} L = 0.59M - 2.44$ (Wells and Coppersmith, 1994); depth of the
  mainshock $\pm 3$~km; magnitudes from a law with $b = 1.30$ truncated 1.2 units
  below the mainshock (B{\aa}th, 1965). \\
Station networks & Jittered grids on New Zealand land (Natural Earth 1:10m):
  GeoNet-like, $0.36^{\circ} \times 0.47^{\circ}$ ($\sim$40~km), 166 stations over
  the whole country; Agency~B, $0.27^{\circ} \times 0.35^{\circ}$ ($\sim$30~km),
  67 stations in $175.5^{\circ}$--$178.6^{\circ}$E, $41.4^{\circ}$--$37.6^{\circ}$S. \\
Recording & A station records an earthquake if its hypocentral distance is below
  $R(M) = 10^{1.11 + 0.38M}$~km times a log-normal station factor
  ($\sigma_{\ln} = 0.25$); an agency catalogues every earthquake that at least
  four of its stations record.  Each recording station adds a P phase, and an S
  phase with probability 0.6. \\
Reported metadata & From the recording stations: used-station and phase counts,
  azimuthal gap (largest gap between their azimuths), nearest-station distance
  $\Delta_{\min}$.  Horizontal uncertainty
  $\sigma_h = 0.9\sqrt{6/n}\,e^{\max(0,\,\mathrm{gap}-150^{\circ})/70^{\circ}}
  (1 + \Delta_{\min}/80\,\mathrm{km})\,\epsilon$~km; depth uncertainty
  $\sigma_z = 1.6\,\sigma_h\,(1 + \Delta_{\min}/(z + 5\,\mathrm{km}))\,\epsilon$,
  with the depth fixed (operator assigned: 12~km GeoNet-like, 10~km Agency~B) and
  no depth uncertainty reported where $\sigma_z > 20$~km; origin-time uncertainty
  $0.05 + \epsilon\,\sigma_h/7$~s; RMS residual with median 0.20~s (GeoNet-like)
  or 0.14~s (Agency~B), times $(1 + \Delta_{\min}/200\,\mathrm{km})$; magnitude
  stations $n_{\mathrm{amp}} \approx 0.6$--$0.95\,n$ and magnitude uncertainty
  $0.05 + 0.30/\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{amp}}}$.  Each $\epsilon$ is an independent
  log-normal factor ($\sigma_{\ln} = 0.25$; 0.2 for the time uncertainty), and
  the RMS residual varies log-normally about its median with
  $\sigma_{\ln} = 0.3$. \\
Reported values & True epicentre, depth, origin time and magnitude plus errors of
  exactly the reported size; $M_L$, evaluation mode manual and status reviewed for
  both agencies; magnitudes to full precision (GeoNet-like) or to 0.1
  (Agency~B). \\

\section*{Statistical analysis in the browser}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/app_analytics_gr_c.png}
  \caption{Gutenberg-Richter analysis of the reduced GeoNet-like catalogue,
  computed client-side with the default $M_c$ settings (maximum curvature plus
  $0.2$).  The platform reports $\hat{b}$ with its counting-statistics
  uncertainty, the $a$-value, the goodness of fit and $M_c$; the
  frequency-magnitude plot marks the range below $M_c$ and overlays the fitted
  relation.  Above $M_c = M1.8 \pm 0.1$ the fit gives
  $\hat{b} = 0.882 \pm 0.019$ ($N = 2{,}151$; $a = 4.92$, $R^2 = 0.963$).  As in
  the worked example of the main paper, this lies below the $b = 1.00$ planted in
  the synthetic background, because the synthetic networks are not complete at
  $M1.8$ offshore and at depth: a single global $M_c$ is set by the best-covered
  part of the catalogue.  The magnitudes are reported to full precision, so no
  binning correction is applied.  The capture is a check of the platform's
  behaviour on a controlled input, not a measurement of New Zealand seismicity.}
  \label{fig:s_gr}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/app_analytics_mc.png}
  \caption{Magnitude-of-completeness panel for the same catalogue, with the
  default settings (maximum curvature, correction $+0.2$; the goodness-of-fit
  test is the alternative method, and the correction can be set from 0 to 0.5).
  The fullest bin of the non-cumulative distribution is $M1.6$, so
  $M_c = M1.8 \pm 0.1$ (one bin width, a lower bound on its uncertainty), and
  $43.7\,\%$ of the events ($2{,}151$ of $4{,}922$) lie at or above it.  That
  share is not a completeness score: for a perfectly complete catalogue with
  $b = 1$ the $+0.2$ correction alone would limit it to about $63\,\%$.  The
  estimate is shown with its method and correction, so that the completeness
  assumption behind any subsequent $b$-value is visible at the point of use.}
  \label{fig:s_mc}
\end{figure}

\section*{Event-level quality index}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/app_analytics_quality_c.png}
  \caption{Catalogue-level quality summary for the same catalogue.  The grade
  distribution and the mean score ($67.9$) are the five-dimension per-event index
  of equation~1 of the main paper, which scores location, network geometry,
  solution, magnitude, and evaluation status; every event is scored when it is
  stored and mapped to an A\textsuperscript{+}--F grade.  The synthetic reports
  carry the fields equation~1 reads (horizontal, depth and origin-time
  uncertainties, azimuthal gap, station and phase counts, RMS residual,
  magnitude uncertainty and station count, and an analyst-reviewed evaluation
  status), so the grades reflect the recording geometry: 86 events ($1.7\,\%$)
  reach A\textsuperscript{+}, most events are graded B ($32.4\,\%$) or C
  ($28.9\,\%$), and the lowest grades go to offshore and small events recorded
  by few stations.  The field-completeness bars are a separate catalogue-level
  metric: $90.6\,\%$ of the events have a location uncertainty in the sense of
  the ``With Uncertainty Data'' card (a latitude, longitude or depth
  uncertainty), the others having a fixed depth and only a horizontal
  uncertainty, and none has a focal mechanism, which equation~1 does not use.}
  \label{fig:s_quality}
\end{figure}
